What an IMEI block actually is

It happens at the network

Every handset carries an IMEI. PTA's DIRBS system can shut that identity off on Pakistani networks, so no SIM works in the phone. That switch sits with the regulator and the operators — not with a shop.

What it was built for

Stolen or lost handsets, and sets that were never registered at all. Debt collection is not what the system is for, and an unpaid balance appears nowhere in its records.

The defaulter policy is not in force

A policy to block the phones of instalment defaulters has been discussed for years, but no such facility is open to shops today. Your recovery cannot wait on it.

Why the “report it stolen” shortcut backfires

Plenty of shops do it anyway — report the handset as stolen to get it blocked. Four reasons it costs more than it recovers.

  1. The report is in your name

    The phone was not stolen — it was sold, and payment stopped. Reporting an incident that did not happen is an offence in itself, and your own agreement, receipt and ledger are the proof that a sale took place.

  2. The phone keeps working anyway

    A block stops the SIM. On Wi-Fi the handset carries on exactly as before, and the customer still holds the set you were never paid for.

  3. It is pressure, not recovery

    Even when the customer pays, the block does not lift itself — getting it removed is a separate round of paperwork, and those days are yours to lose. A shop running instalments needs something automatic, not one application per customer.

  4. Your shop's name

    Word travels in a market. One false report sends your next twenty instalment customers to the shop down the road.

What works instead: a lock on the same IMEI

A block is applied from outside; a lock sits inside the handset. At the counter the phone scans a QR, the customer signs the agreement on screen, and the lock is stamped onto that phone's own IMEI.

Common questions

Can a shop get a customer's IMEI blocked for non-payment?

Not for non-payment. A PTA block exists for stolen, lost and unregistered handsets, not for recovering a debt, and a shop has no way to apply one. The route that is open to you is a lock the customer agreed to in the signed agreement.

Qist na dene par phone block ho sakta hai?

PTA wala block nahin hota, lekin phone lock ho jata hai. Tareekh guzarne aur grace period khatam hone par set khud lock hota hai, aur payment darj hote hi khud khul jata hai.

What is the difference between a block and a lock?

A block acts at the network and stops the SIM; the handset still runs on Wi-Fi. A lock acts inside the handset: the whole screen stops, with or without data, and releases itself when the instalment is paid.

Customer ne phone reset kar diya to lock khatam ho jayega?

Nahin. Settings se reset band rehta hai, aur jab tak raqam baqaya hai, recovery se wipe kiya hua phone bhi aap ka Google account mangta hai. Wohi phone dobara enrol kare to purana lock wapas jurr jata hai, naya lock kharch nahin hota.

Does the customer have to agree to the lock?

Yes, and that is the whole basis of it. The agreement names the handset, its IMEI, every instalment date, the grace period and the plain statement that the phone may be restricted if payment stops. The customer signs on screen, and without that signature no lock is applied.

Kya aap customer ki location track karte hain ya data mitate hain?

Phone ka musalsal peecha nahin kiya jata, aur remote wipe bilkul nahin — customer ki photos aur data koi nahin mita sakta. Sirf locked ya overdue phone ki ek baar location, agar agreement ijazat de, aur customer ko bataya jata hai.

Does it work on any Android handset?

On a new Android handset, enrolled from the QR at the moment of sale. A phone already in use has to be reset to enrol. This method does not work on iPhone.

Qeemat kya hai?

Per phone ek baar Rs 600 se Rs 800, pack ke hisaab se. Koi mahana fees nahin, aur ek hi phone par doosra lock kabhi kharch nahin hota.
