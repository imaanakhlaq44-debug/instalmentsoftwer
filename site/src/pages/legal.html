Legal

The agreement, the data, and the limits.

Locking someone's phone is a serious thing. This page states what the customer signs, what is stored, and what the software refuses to let a dealer do.

What the customer signs

Every financed sale produces a written agreement stating: the handset's brand, model and IMEI; the total, the down payment and the balance; every instalment date and amount; the grace period; any late fee; and that the phone may be restricted if payment is not made after that grace period. Clause 4 also states, where the shop uses them: the offline rule, staged restrictions, location for recovery, the hardware checks and the reset protection described below.

The agreement is in both Urdu and English, and the customer signs it on screen. A copy goes to the customer.

The terms are versioned (currently v1.4). A published version is never edited, and every agreement is shown under the version it was signed with. Whatever a customer's signed version does not say, the software does not do to that customer's phone — until they sign a newer agreement.

What the software refuses a dealer

  • Locking a phone whose agreement is unsigned
  • Locking a phone whose agreement has been voided
  • Locking a phone whose plan figures changed after signing
  • Locking before the grace period has run out
  • Seeing or touching another shop's customers or handsets

Every lock, unlock, payment and edit is written to an audit trail, naming the person who did it.

What data is held

The customer's name, phone number, CNIC, address and emergency contact; the handset's brand, model, IMEI and status; the instalment and payment record; and the agreement with its signature.

The handset app does not read or send the customer's messages, call history, contacts, photos or files.

On every contact (about every fifteen minutes, when it has internet) the phone reports: battery level, Android version and security-patch date, mobile network name, app version, whether a command was applied, and whether it restricted itself under the offline rule. The server also records the time of contact and the IP address it came from.

Location and hardware information are also collected, but only in the circumstances set out in the two sections below.

Location, for recovering a phone

The handset app can report the phone's location. It is not continuous tracking, and it is off unless the shop turns it on. All of these must hold before a location is taken: the customer signed an agreement (terms v1.3 or later) that says so, while the shop had it switched on; the phone is restricted, or an instalment is unpaid after its grace period; and the shop's owner asked for it, giving a reason.

Each request produces one location, not a trail. The request stays open for 24 hours and closes as soon as the phone answers or the customer pays. The phone shows the customer a notice every time its location is shared. The app holds the location permission only for the moment of that one reading.

Only the most recent location is stored. Only the shop's owner can see it — not counter staff — and every request and every viewing is written to the audit trail. A customer whose instalments are up to date is never located, and the stored location is deleted when the phone is released.

Hardware checks and tampering

So that the phone securing an agreement cannot be swapped or altered, the app reads the handset's IMEI numbers and serial number and checks whether its system software has been modified — rooted, bootloader unlocked, or running unofficial firmware. Where the phone supports it, its secure chip signs a statement of the same facts about once a day. These are reported with each contact and shown to the shop's owner.

If serious tampering is found — the IMEI or serial no longer matches the agreement, the phone is rooted, or its bootloader is unlocked — the phone may be restricted even though no instalment is overdue, until the shop has inspected it. This is the one restriction that does not depend on a missed payment.

These checks and that restriction are written into the agreement from terms v1.4. For a customer who signed an earlier version, the server discards these reports without storing them and does not restrict the phone for tampering.

Protection against resetting the phone

While money is owed, the phone cannot be factory-reset from its settings, the management app cannot be uninstalled, and USB debugging and installing apps from outside the app store are switched off. These are applied by the app on every managed phone from the day it is set up.

If the shop has set it up, the phone is also tied to the shop's own Google account: after being erased from the recovery menu it will not finish setting up until the shop signs in. The shop's account is sent only to a phone whose customer signed terms v1.4 or later, and it is removed once the last instalment is paid, so a customer who owns the phone outright never needs the shop to use it.

Who can see it

A shop owner sees everything in their own shop. CNIC, IMEI and address are hidden from counter staff — not merely masked on screen, but never sent at all. A customer sees only their own record.

Limits on the lock

A locked phone keeps emergency calling, keeps showing what is owed and how to pay, and unlocks itself as soon as a verified payment arrives. Nothing on the phone is erased — the app has no wipe command.

Where the shop uses staged restrictions (terms v1.2 or later), one missed instalment stops outgoing calls and SMS, a second also pauses social, messaging and video apps, and only a third — or the final instalment — locks the phone. Incoming and emergency calls work at every stage.

Where the shop uses the offline rule (terms v1.1 or later), a phone kept off the internet for the number of days printed on the agreement restricts itself — but only if an instalment is also genuinely overdue. A paid-up phone without signal is left alone.

How long it is kept

When a phone is paid off or released, the app gives the phone back completely and the server deletes its stored location, hardware reports and tamper record. The customer's details, the agreement with its signature, the payment record and the audit trail are kept by the shop as its business records; the software does not currently delete them automatically after any fixed period.

This page describes how the software behaves. It is not legal advice, and the agreement's terms were drafted by engineers and are awaiting review by a Pakistani lawyer — have your own lawyer review the instalment agreement your shop uses.
